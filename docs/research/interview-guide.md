# Wishlist walkthrough — moderator guide

**6 participants · 45 minutes · screen-share required, camera optional.**

Recruited from the survey's Q11 opt-in. This is a *walkthrough*, not a Q&A: the
participant opens their real Myntra wishlist and narrates it item by item. People
recall almost nothing accurately about why they saved something in general, and
explain a specific item in front of them very well.

**What it has to produce**, because Part 4 is blocked on exactly two things:

| Output | Feeds |
|---|---|
| 60–100 coded items across 6 people | Part 3 frequency table, and the triangulation against Part 1 |
| A **laddered** root cause, not a stated one | Part 4 (c) |
| Workarounds people actually used | Part 4 (d) — our thinnest section |
| A live test of the falsification condition | Part 4 (c), and honesty on the deck |

## Before the call

- [ ] Recording consent asked **in writing at opt-in and again out loud on the call.** Without a recording or near-verbatim notes you cannot code six 30-minute calls, and coding is the deliverable.
- [ ] Confirm they are on Myntra and have items saved (the survey already screened both — re-confirm anyway, Forms branching can be misread).
- [ ] Transcript tool running. Otter/Fireflies, or Meet's own captions.
- [ ] Coding sheet open in a second window with their `P#` row block pre-created.
- [ ] Say plainly: not affiliated with Myntra, nothing is being sold, they can stop at any point, no wrong answers.

**Say this before the scroll:** *"I'm going to ask you to open your wishlist and
talk me through it. There's no right answer and I'm not judging anything in
there — the messier it is the more useful this is."* People are mildly
embarrassed by their own wishlist and will otherwise curate what they show you.

## 0–5 min · Warm-up — decision style

Not throat-clearing. It establishes how this person decides *before* they are
thinking about wishlists, so you can tell later whether the wishlist behaviour is
specific or just who they are.

1. What's the last piece of clothing you bought online? Walk me through how you decided.
2. How long between first seeing it and actually paying?
3. Was there a moment you nearly didn't buy it? What happened?

## 5–12 min · The unprompted scroll — and the surface itself

**"Open your wishlist, share your screen, and just scroll from the top. React out
loud — whatever comes into your head."**

Then say nothing. This produces the most quotable material in the whole study and
it only survives if you don't fill the silence.

Capture, without prompting for them:
- The first spontaneous reaction (*"oh god, why do I have all this"* is the common one — record the exact words)
- Total item count, and roughly how far back the oldest goes
- Whether they scroll fast past a block of items, and where they slow down

Then two questions:
4. If you had to delete half of this right now, which half goes?
5. When did you last open this list on your own, without me asking?

### The surface itself — **watch, don't ask**

Added after a review found this gap: nothing in the project tested it. Part 2 §2.4
justifies not attacking term B on the grounds that **"revisit is already served by
notifications and price-drop alerts."** That is one of the three legs the choice of
term C stands on, and it has been an assumption throughout. This is where it gets
checked.

The teardown says what is *on* the Myntra wishlist. Only this block says what gets
*used*. Their screen is already shared and they are already scrolling — so most of
this is observation, and costs no interview time.

**Observe silently during the scroll above. Tick what you actually see:**

- [ ] Touches the **category chips** at all — and if so, what they were looking for
- [ ] Reacts to a **PRICE DROP** badge — pauses, taps, or scrolls straight past
- [ ] Notices the **cashback / promo banner** above the first product, or blanks it
- [ ] Uses **share** on any card
- [ ] Uses **move to bag** — and whether it is the item they were discussing
- [ ] Any pinch, zoom, or long-press on a product photo *(a fit-inspection tell)*
- [ ] Sorting or reordering behaviour — or visible frustration that none exists

**Then two questions, and only two:**

6. **What brought you back to this list the last few times — do you remember?**
   → If they say a notification or a price drop: *which one, and did you buy?*
   This is the term-B question. "Came back because of a price alert and still
   didn't buy" is the strongest single sentence available for Part 2 §2.4.

7. **Is there anything on this screen you've never used?**
   → Ask *after* the scroll, never before — naming a feature makes them use it.

**Do not ask** whether they like the wishlist, whether it is easy to use, or what
they would add. Stated preference on a surface they open twice a month is worth
nothing, and "what would you add" produces a feature request, not a problem. If
they volunteer one, write it down and move on — do not follow it.

add it to the tick-list before the first call so it is checked consistently.

## 12–32 min · Item-by-item walkthrough — **the core**

**5–8 items per participant.** Let them pick the first two, then you pick: one
they scrolled past fast, one they lingered on, and the oldest item in the list.
Self-selected items are the ones with a tidy story attached; the ones they skipped
are where the real behaviour is.

Run the same eight probes on each item. Fill one coding-sheet row per item as you go.

1. Take me back — where were you and what were you doing when you saved this?
2. What was going through your head? What was it *for*?
3. Do you still want it, 0–10? → **"Why not 10?"** and **"Why not lower?"** (both directions; the gap is the doubt)
4. **What exactly is stopping you right now?** → then ladder: *"and what's behind that?"* **× 3**
5. If you had to decide in the next 60 seconds — buy or delete? **What would you need to know to be sure?**
6. Did you look at anything outside Myntra about this? What, where, why?
7. Is there another item you're weighing this against? Show me.
8. Last time you were unsure like this and *did* buy — what tipped you?

### On the laddering (probe 4) — this is the part that earns Part 4 (c)

"I wasn't sure about the size" is a **stated blocker**, not a root cause. It goes
in the `stated_blocker` column and nowhere else. Keep going:

> *"I wasn't sure about the size."*
> — And what's behind that? *"Their sizes are all over the place brand to brand."*
> — And what's behind that? *"I've been burned before, I got a shirt that was two sizes off."*
> — And what's behind that? *"Sending it back is a whole thing and I'd rather not own it than deal with that."*

Three levels down is a returns-friction root cause wearing a fit costume. That
distinction is the difference between an MVP that shows a size chart and one that
resolves the doubt. **Do not stop early because the first answer sounded complete.**

Stop at three, or when the answer starts repeating — whichever comes first. If
they say "I don't know", sit in the silence for five seconds before rescuing them.

laddering line for it here so you push on it consistently rather than only when

## 32–40 min · Workarounds & outside-the-app behaviour

Part 4 (d) rests entirely on this block. `external_behaviour` reads 2.2% in the
corpus and STATUS.md already concedes that is an artefact of scraped text — people
narrate outcomes, not process. This is the only instrument that will evidence it.

**Ask about the past, never the future.** "Do you ever..." invites a polite yes.
"When was the last time you..." forces an actual memory or an actual no.

- When did you last screenshot something from Myntra? What did you do with it — which app, who did you send it to, what did they say back?
- Last time you went hunting for reviews — where, and what were you looking for specifically? (photos? someone your build? a particular brand?)
- Have you ever ordered two sizes meaning to return one? Walk me through the last time.
- Ever seen something here and then bought it in a store — or the reverse?
- Do you keep track of things you want anywhere *other* than the wishlist? Show me that.

For each one that lands: **what did it cost you** (time, favours, the return trip)
and **did it actually resolve the question**. A workaround that didn't work is as
informative as one that did.

`[SHARPEN]` — survey Q8's option list, and the Q9 free text, will name workarounds
nobody listed here. Add them as named probes before call 1 rather than
discovering them in call 5.

## 40–45 min · The falsification question, then close

**Ask this of every participant, in these words, before any concept stimulus:**

> *"Thinking about everything still in that list — how much of it is stuff you're
> genuinely unsure about, versus stuff you know you want and are just waiting on
> something for? Money, an occasion, a sale, the season?"*

**This can end the project's current direction, and that is the point.** Part 4 (c)
argues that the wishlist stores the item and discards the *question*. If people
know exactly why each item is saved and are simply waiting for payday or a
wedding, that articulation is wrong — the problem is timing, not doubt, and the
MVP has to change. Record the answer verbatim and count it across all six.

Do not soften it, do not ask it last thing while they're putting on a coat, and
do not follow it with a question that fishes for the answer you want.

**Then, time permitting only** — 2–3 concept stimuli, described in one sentence
each, for directional signal on the MVP. Reactions here are the weakest data you
will collect; treat them as such and never let them override the walkthrough.

**Close:** anything I should have asked and didn't? Thank them. Tell them where
the finding will end up.

## Moderator rules

1. **Never ask "would you use this?"** People say yes to be kind, and the answer predicts nothing. Ask what they did last time.
2. **Five seconds of silence after every answer.** The second sentence is the true one. This is the single highest-yield habit in the guide.
3. **Ask about the past, not the future or the hypothetical.**
4. **Do not explain your hypothesis**, at any point, including when they ask what the study is about. Say "why saved things don't get bought" and leave it there.
5. **Their words go in the sheet, not your paraphrase.** `evidence_quote` must be verbatim or it is not a quote.
6. **Time-box the warm-up hard.** It runs long by default and the item-by-item block is what produces the deliverable.
7. **If they only have 3 items**, do all three properly rather than padding. Note the low count — a short wishlist is itself a finding.
