Myntra · 30-day wishlist→purchase conversion

Shoppers do come back. They come back through search —
and the wishlist behaves as if the save never happened.

19,143 documents said the problem was fit and trust. 16 survey responses said the failure happens one step earlier — at re-entry. Both are true; the discovery engine was structurally unable to see the second one. The MVP is built for the moment the engine was blind to, and it spends information rather than money.

THE METRIC

W30 = A·quality of save × B·re-entry × C·doubt resolution × D·mechanics. We attack B-self × C — the only pair that is unowned and permitted by the no-incentive constraint.

THE BUILD

A "From your Wishlist" module that fires only on expressed search intent. Live: 546 tests, 10 measured acceptance gates, 5 experiment arms, a validated 30-day cohort model.
THE THREAD
W30 decomposed → 19,143 docs, 12 themes → survey n=16 + 6 walkthroughs → "the wishlist stores the item and discards the question" → search-reconnection MVP → +15% relative W30, guarded by return rate

THE ARITHMETIC — AND THE ARGUMENT THAT SURVIVES SCRUTINY

W30 = 0.722 × 0.45 × 0.34 × 0.85 = 9.4%   →   C: 0.34 → 0.44  →  12.2%  (+2.8pp, +29% rel.)
"C has the most leverage" is arithmetically false — in a multiplicative model a +25% relative lift on any term moves W30 by +25%. The case is headroom (C sits near 34%, D near 85%), ownership (B-scheduled and D are already optimised), and the fact that the monetary ban removes D's dominant friction outright.

WHAT WE DROPPED, AND WHY THAT MATTERS

Return anxiety read as a top-three blocker at 11.3% full-corpus. Restricted to Myntra and to pre-purchase it is 1.9% — returns are what people complain about after, not what stops them buying. We dropped it. Fit moved the other way on the same correction: 21.1% → 30.6% of pre-purchase documents, and 46.2% of named doubts.
The number that undermines the ranking: only 1.56% of tagged documents mention a wishlist at all. The engine ranked what people write about — and nobody writes a review about the item they forgot they saved.

Primary research · the reconciliation

The engine measured complaint volume; primary research asked about the moment of return.

CONFIRMED

  • Fit-adjacent doubt is real and broad. Fabric, photos, "suit my body", fit — together they touch 8 of 14 (57%) wishlists (Q12).
  • Doubt genuinely goes unresolved. 21% answered "I tried, and still couldn't find out" (Q8) — the first direct measurement of term C, which the corpus could not produce.
  • Two doubts, not one population. Fit×trust overlap is 1 of 14 — "one shopper, mislabelled" is not supported.

CHALLENGED

  • Asked for one blocker, shoppers do not say fit. Price/sale takes 7 of 14 (50%); fit + trust + fabric take 4 of 14 (29%) (Q7, single-select).
  • A blocker the corpus never surfaced: "I'd forgotten about it until now."
  • The failure is shaped like comparison, not ignorance. 36% buy none of the close alternatives or leave to find more; 14% buy none outright (Q11).
  • W30 finally has a measured direction: 14% bought from the wishlist in 30 days (Q4).

WHY BOTH RESULTS ARE TRUE — WHAT DIFFERS IS THE INSTRUMENT, NOT THE FINDING

The corpus is reviews, comments and posts — written after delivery, about the object. It sees a doubt once, never twice, and 1.56% of it mentions a wishlist. "Passive Wishlist" scored 12th on 0.33% reach not because the behaviour is rare, but because nobody narrates it.
The survey asked about a specific saved item, now. At that moment price salience wins the single-select, fit-adjacent doubt is broader than any one label, and the dominant failure is a decision left open. Term C is the content of the doubt; B-self is whether it is ever re-opened. The engine ranked C well and could not see B at all.
So the MVP does not choose between them. It fires at self-initiated re-entry (B-self) and carries the saved decision back with enough evidence to close it (C) — the one place both terms are addressable with no discount and no notification.
Method and limits, stated once: survey instrument and coding rules published with the dashboard · 16 responses → 15 valid shoppers → 14 item-level, plus 6 moderated walkthroughs. At n=14 one respondent moves any share ~7pp — directional confirmation of corpus-scale findings, not proof.

THE SURFACE TODAY — TEARDOWN

A flat reverse-chronological grid with no memory of why anything was saved — plus a price-drop alert, a discount badge, a cashback banner and a coupon. Every lever is monetary.

WHY THE HARDEST CONSTRAINT IS ACTUALLY THE INSTRUCTION

A discount does not reduce risk — it prices it and leaves it exactly where it was. Every lever on this surface today is monetary, and every one leaves the item sitting there. Cash on Delivery is the precedent, for the framing not the feature: framed as "low card penetration" it buys a payments integration; framed as "the customer carries all the risk upfront" it buys COD. Re-allocate the risk rather than discount the price of bearing it.
Falsification, pre-committed: if the walkthroughs describe not knowing rather than not wanting to be wrong, level 5 is unsupported — we keep level 3, drop the risk framing, and say which read produced that. A workaround named by 4 of 6 goes on a slide; named once, it stays in the appendix.

Solution rationale · the MVP

Search is where the decision re-opens, so the wishlist meets them there.

1 · "DID I SAVE THIS?"
Fires only on expressed search intent. Saved colour and size, two co-equal actions.
2 · "STILL RIGHT FOR ME?"
Ten signals, each citing a source. Fit reads INFO, never a score.
3 · "WHAT ELSE MATTERS?"
A priority reorders and never filters. No winner is named.

CONSTRAINTS, ENFORCED AT LINT LEVEL

C-1 no discount, coupon, cashback or sale urgency · C-2 no push in v1 · C-3 search renders independently, fail-open in ≤250 ms · C-4 precision > recall · C-6 logged-out sessions reveal nothing · C-7 accessibility is a launch gate.

WHAT WE DELIBERATELY DID NOT BUILD

A fit score — permanently status: "unknown". No size chart or fit feedback exists here, so a score would be invented data wearing the costume of evidence. Also unbuilt and named on the surface: review quality as distinct from count, seller trust, stock depth, visual similarity.

Risks & mitigation

The largest risk is not that the feature fails. It is that the experiment cannot tell us whether it did.

RiskWhy it is real — measured, not fearedMitigation, pre-committed
R1 · The panel cannot answer the question A 400-tester panel supports only a 70.66pp lift. The real 5pp lift needs 19,390; the B−A question needs 1,22,787. Five arms, intent-to-treat dilution, a between-treatment contrast. Re-scope the panel to what it can answer: comprehension, recovery from an unavailable variant, whether the 10 states read. Run the powered A/B on real traffic only, and power for B−A or drop the B arm.
R2 · The evidence is thinnest where the decision was made The corpus cannot see re-entry — 1.56% of it mentions a wishlist. The instrument that can is n=14 item-level, where one respondent moves a share ~7pp. Falsification tallies fixed before the sessions: a workaround named by 4 of 6 earns a slide; if mostly_waiting wins, the problem is timing and the direction changes.
R3 · A false positive trains shoppers to ignore the module A wrong "you saved this" is worse than a miss, and unrecoverable — the next correct one is not believed either. Exact matching only, no embeddings in v1. Thresholds keyed per modality (0.72 text / 0.82 voice / 0.85 image). Below threshold: render nothing. Dismissal suppresses and logs a signal.
R4 · Our own gate numbers flatter us Precision labels are generator-derived, and the generator infers brand and type the way the matcher does — a shared error is invisible. Test queries carry no typos. Quoted as regression guards, not launch evidence. Before ramp: a hand-labelled 500-pair set, queries sampled from real traffic, a 500 rps load test.
R5 · Honest answers cost short-term GMV Remove/archive rises by design, and resolving a doubt toward "no" books zero revenue this quarter. Measured as 60/90-day repeat purchase rate in the treatment cell, with the direction declared in advance so it cannot be renegotiated at read-out.
R6 · We contradicted our own pre-registered rule Our guardrail said "this corpus does not support a wishlist-UI-centric MVP." The shipped MVP is one, and fit in it is permanently unknown. Amended with the reasoning, dated — not quietly dropped. The rule was right about the corpus and wrong about its coverage.
Privacy is a launch blocker, not a risk row: wishlist data never crosses the account boundary. Gate: 0 wishlist fields across 66 unauthenticated calls, covering response payloads and the shadow topic — timing side-channels are not covered and need their own pass before ramp.

In closing

The engine found the doubt. Shoppers found the moment. The build is instrumented to be proved wrong.

AI DISCOVERY

PRIMARY RESEARCH
n=16 plus 6 walkthroughs. Confirmed the doubt, overturned the moment, and exposed the corpus's blind spot.
PROBLEM
The wishlist stores the item and discards the question — and the shopper carries the risk of being wrong.
MVP
Search-reconnection, live. Memory plus agency; no money, no notification.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY BUILT AND RUNNING

  • Discovery engine, deployed with a live cohort toggle and a working classify endpoint — dashboard
  • Prototype, 546 tests, 10 measured gates, all 10 module states reachable — live link
  • The measurement pipeline is itself validated: the staged ramp held assignment across 13,182 users with 0 arm changes, the kill switch fired on a 74.5% guardrail breach, and sequential inference held its error rate where fixed-horizon peeking did not.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

If the walkthroughs describe not knowing rather than not wanting to be wrong, the risk framing goes and level 3 stands alone. If mostly_waiting wins the tally, the problem is timing and this is the wrong build. If L2 is high and L3 is flat, the module is seen and useless — that is a kill, not an iteration.
In one line: a save is an open question, and the only non-monetary moment to close it is the next time the shopper asks it themselves.
Limits carried to the end, not buried: no absolute term baselines until day 1 of internal instrumentation · n=14 item-level survey · gate numbers are generated-label regression guards · no real fit score, review-quality or seller-trust signal, and no visual similarity · the recruited panel answers usability, not lift.
