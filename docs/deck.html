The MVP acts on B × C jointly, at one moment

Term C alone has the most headroom, but a doubt only gets a second chance to resolve if the shopper comes back. Self-initiated re-search is the one revisit path nothing today serves — not a notification, not a price alert, both of which are either owned by someone else or banned outright. The reconnection module fires exactly there: the moment a shopper searches again is the moment B and C can be closed in the same interaction.

Why Myntra-only, and why fit rises

23% of the raw corpus is AJIO/Nykaa, and it inflates returns 6.3pp. Excluding it swaps ranks 2 and 3. Fit moves the other way — 19.5% Myntra-only, strongest in YouTube at 24.3%, the source closest to pre-purchase deliberation. That is the defensible headline, not returns.

What we ruled out, with numbers

  • Styling/occasion: 0.6% and 0.2% of named doubts — fully expressible in the taxonomy throughout, so this is a finding, not a blind spot
  • Theme 12 (passive wishlist): predicted "most important" from 40 docs; confirmed rarest at 18 docs / 0.5%, lowest judged proximity
  • Direct wishlist-UI evidence: ~60 docs, ~1.5% of the corpus — this corpus cannot demonstrate a wishlist-screen-shaped fix

Part 3 — Primary research

Six walkthroughs, testing two things the corpus could not see

What corpus evidence can't reach

  • Whether the doubt is still unresolved at revisit, or resolved and declined — the corpus sees it once, never twice
  • Whether fit-uncertain (28.3%, 3.99× lift) and low-trust (20.9%, 2.81× lift) shoppers are one population or two — tagger assigned one segment signal per doc, so overlap is a labelling artefact
  • Whether anything today actually brings a shopper back to the wishlist (term B) — assumed "served by notifications," never measured

Pre-registered falsification test

Root-cause ladder, probe 4, tallied across 6 sessions: if mostly_waiting wins over mostly_unsure, the problem is timing, not doubt, and the direction changes. Within mostly_unsure: not knowing → information feature; not wanting to be wrong → risk feature (this MVP). Decided in advance, not read afterward.

Part 4 — Problem definition

The wishlist accumulates decisions. It never closes one.

L1 · Symptom
Wishlisted items don't get bought.
The brief
L2 · Blocker
"I'm not sure it'll fit." 66.1% name an unresolved doubt; 46.2% of those are fit.
Measured
L3 · Mechanism
The wishlist records the SKU and discards the question. Return visits meet a reverse-chronological grid with no memory of why anything was saved.
Measured — teardown
L4 · Why unanswerable
The answer that would settle it is never joined to this shopper at the moment they're asking again.
Hypothesis — §5
L5 · Root cause
The shopper carries the entire risk of being wrong — sizing is seller-supplied and inconsistent, every cost of a bad buy lands on them.

Leading with fit — larger signal, higher lift, closest to purchase intent.

Why "no monetary incentive" is the instruction, not the handicap

A discount doesn't reduce risk, it prices it and leaves it exactly where it was. The constraint and the root cause point at the same build: resolve the doubt honestly, at the moment the shopper is already asking again.

07 / 10
Part 5 — Solution & MVP

Reconnect at the search moment. Answer with named evidence, or say "unknown."

  • Reconnection module — a saved match resurfaces in search results with saved variant, availability and delivery, cited to source
  • Decision Confidence Layer — ten signals, each with a "Why." status: "unknown" is a first-class outcome: this catalog has no size chart, so fit says exactly that rather than inventing a score
  • Comparison re-entry — a resumed comparison names what changed since last time, never silently re-renders stale data
  • Cross-category pairing — drawn only from the shopper's own wishlist, never the catalog; kept pending the T6 interview probe on styling

The test this MVP has to pass

"If the user reads our output and still doesn't know whether it fits, we built a message." This MVP passes by being honest rather than confident: it answers what the catalog can support (availability, delivery, saved-variant status) and refuses to fake what it can't (a fit score). It removes the uncertainty it can, and names the uncertainty it can't — neither one is a reminder.

The counter-metric, named in advance

Remove/archive rate rises in treatment, and that's a success — closing a decision negatively is still closing it. An engine that never says "this isn't for you" is a salesperson, and gets read as one.

The decision rule that matters most

L1 high, L2 flat → we built a message. That is the real falsification, decided before the read-out, not argued about after it.

What we chose not to build, and why that's a risk we accepted on purpose

No fit score (data can't support one honestly), no image similarity search, no notification-based re-engagement. Each removes a lever a competitor might reach for first — accepted because building any of them would mean shipping invented data or the CS1 mistake.
